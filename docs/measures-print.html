CHiP-D measures · what each one is, how it is calculated, and what decides compliance

A tabulated expansion of cd-compliance-pipeline.canvas. Every formula and every threshold below was read out of the code, not out of the papers — the reference is given so the two can be checked against each other.

Thresholds shown are the values live in config.yaml on this machine. A printable version is generated alongside this file as measures-print.html.

Notation. a = the acceleration vector of one raw sample. SVM = the per-epoch signal vector magnitude. X[d,h] = the day-by-hour activity matrix. X̄ = a grand mean. All logarithms are base 10.

The one thing to carry forward: SVM_sum is a sum, not a rate. Anything downstream that compares it against an absolute number is epoch-length dependent; anything that takes a ratio of its variability is not. The columns on the right of every table below are an application of exactly that rule.

3 · Step 2 — circadian and sleep metrics

actigraphy-sleep-metrics · <stem>_60s.csv → non-parametric, daily, periodogram and SRI CSVs. Step 2 is not modified by anything in this project.

3.1 The aggregation every measure below sits on

StepWhat happensFormula
Hourly activityEach epoch is floored to its calendar hour and averaged.X[d,h] = mean(SVM_sum over epochs in hour h of day d)
Day × hour matrixOne row per calendar day, 24 columns, NaN where there is no recording.shape (D, 24)

The matrix shape is the same at any epoch length — 30 s simply averages 120 epochs per hour instead of 60. But the level of X[d,h] halves, because SVM_sum is a sum. That single fact determines every right-hand column below.

3.4 SRI — sleep regularity index

Computed from the epoch series, not the hourly matrix, so it is the one measure that genuinely depends on epoch resolution.

StageWhat happensFormulaSetting
1 · NormaliseSVM_sum is converted to a per-second rate before scoring.activity_t = SVM_sum_t / epoch_secnormalize_per_second = True (default)
2 · Score sleep/wakeCole–Kripke (1992) automatic scoring.D_t = scale · Σ_{k=−3..3} w_k · activity_{t+k}, w = [106, 54, 58, 76, 230, 74, 67]; epoch is sleep if D_t < 1, else wakescale = 0.001, threshold = 1.0
3 · Align by clock timeBuild a days × epochs-per-day matrix.M = 86400 / epoch_sec columnsepoch_sec is inferred from the data (cli.py:200)
4 · Compare consecutive daysCount epochs that match 24 h apart.SRI = 200 · (matching pairs / valid pairs) − 100pairs with a missing day excluded

Epoch-length behaviour — the precise answer. Two of the three things you might worry about are already handled:

  • the amplitude is handled: stage 1 divides by epoch_sec, so a 30 s per-second rate is directly comparable to a 60 s one. The halving cancels.
  • the grid is handled: epoch_sec is inferred, so M becomes 2880 at 30 s and the clock alignment stays correct. There is no silent collision.
  • what is not handled is the time span of the kernel. Cole–Kripke's seven weights are defined for 1-minute epochs, so at 60 s the window covers 7 minutes of real time; at 30 s it covers 3.5 minutes. The scoring therefore smooths over half as much real time and flips state more readily, which biases SRI downward. sri.py:173 raises a warning whenever epoch_sec ≠ 60.

So a 30 s SRI is not wildly wrong — it is differently smoothed, and the difference between it and the 60 s value is the kernel, not the participant. That is why it must not be exported from a variant without the warning attached.

7 · Epoch-length summary

The single table to consult before interpreting a recompute variant.

Measure30 s vs 60 sWhy
SVM_sum per epochhalvesa sum over half as many samples
Hourly activity levelhalvesmean of epoch sums
IS, IVinvariantvariance ratios
M10 / L5 hourinvariantargmax / argmin
M10 / L5 levelhalvesan activity level
RA, L5:M10 (not computed)would be invariantratios
Chi-square Q_p, p-value, peak periodinvariantvariance ratio
Enright A_p valuehalvesnot variance-normalised
Enright peak periodinvariantargmax
SRIbiased downwardamplitude and grid are handled; the Cole–Kripke kernel spans half the real time
Every compliance thresholdinvalidall absolute; three rows outright wrong — so compliance is not run on a variant

Rule of thumb. Ratios and positions survive a change of epoch length; levels and absolute thresholds do not. Of the measures step 2 reports today, the timing results (M10/L5 hours, peak periods) and the regularity results (IS, IV, Q_p) are directly comparable between a 60 s baseline and a 30 s variant. The levels are not, and SRI is comparable only with its warning attached.
